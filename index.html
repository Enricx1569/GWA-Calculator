The Philippine Grading System

Most Philippine schools use a 1.00–5.00 scale where, unlike GPA systems elsewhere, lower is better — 1.00 is the highest grade and 5.00 means failure. This comes from the old Spanish rank-order system, where 1st place got the number 1.

Not every school uses the same scale, though. Schools like UP and UST use 0.25 increments (1.00, 1.25, 1.50, and so on), while others like DLSU and Ateneo use wider 0.5 increments or a percentage-based system instead. Always check your own school's grading guide before relying on any calculator.

How a Weighted Average Works

GWA multiplies each grade by the units of that subject, adds those products together, then divides by the total units. Subjects worth more units pull the average harder.

Calculus (5 units) at 2.00 → 10.00
Filipino (3 units) at 1.25 → 3.75
PE (2 units) at 1.00 → 2.00

Total grade points: 15.75  ·  Total units: 10
GWA = 15.75 ÷ 10 = 1.58

A straight average of the three grades would have given 1.42 — noticeably better than the real answer, because it lets the 2-unit PE grade count as much as the 5-unit major.

What's a "Grade Increment"?

It's the smallest step your school allows between grades. If your school only gives grades like 1.00, 1.25, 1.50 — never 1.10 or 1.37 — your increment is 0.25. Set this in the calculator so it can round your "grade needed" result to something you could actually receive.

Semester GWA vs. Yearly GWA

Semester GWA only covers subjects taken in one term. Yearly GWA combines both semesters, weighted by each subject's units across the whole school year — not just averaging the two semester GWAs together, since subjects rarely carry the same number of units. This matters for honors computation, since most schools evaluate Latin honors using the full-year or full-program GWA, not a single term.

Latin Honors and Cum Laude

Cum Laude, Magna Cum Laude, and Summa Cum Laude requirements vary by school, but they're almost always based on a minimum overall GWA maintained consistently across every semester — not just your final year. Some schools also require no grade below a certain threshold (for example, no grade lower than 2.00) in any subject, regardless of your overall average. Check your school's specific policy, since a calculator can only tell you the GWA math, not your school's full honors criteria.

Residency rules catch a lot of students by surprise too. Transferees and students who shifted courses are often required to have completed a minimum number of units or years in the school before they can be considered at all.

Common Mistakes When Computing GWA

The most common error is averaging grades directly instead of weighting them by units — a 1-unit PE class and a 5-unit major subject should not count equally. Another common mistake is forgetting that a "4.00" or conditional grade in some schools isn't simply averaged in as-is; it may need to be resolved into a final passing or failing grade first, depending on your school's policy.

Dropped subjects, non-academic subjects like NSTP, and repeated subjects also get handled differently from school to school. Some count the repeat only, some count both attempts. If any of these apply to you, ask your registrar which units actually enter the computation before trusting a number.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I use this calculator?

Set your school's grading scale first (highest, lowest, and passing grade). Then add each subject with its units and grade. Leave the grade blank, or tick "Calculate grade needed," for subjects you haven't finished yet. Click Calculate to see your current or final GWA.

Why does it say my target GWA is "not attainable"?

This means that even if you scored the highest possible grade in every remaining subject, your final GWA still wouldn't reach your target. The calculator shows you the best GWA you could still realistically achieve instead.

Why does it say my target is "already secured"?

This means your target is already guaranteed no matter what happens in your remaining subjects — even the lowest grade on your scale in everything left wouldn't bring you below your target. That's a statement about the average only. You'd still need to actually pass each subject.

My school doesn't use a 1.00–5.00 scale. Can I still use this?

Yes. Change the Highest, Lowest, and Passing Grade fields under Grading Scale to match your school's system — for example, 100 as highest and 65 as lowest for a percentage-based scale. The calculator adjusts its math automatically based on those numbers.

What is the "Grade Increment" field for?

It rounds the "grade you need" result to a number your school would actually give. If your school only issues grades in steps of 0.25 (like 1.00, 1.25, 1.50), set the increment to 0.25 so the result isn't something like "1.43," which no professor would actually mark.

Do my entries stay here if I close the tab?

Yes. Everything is saved in your own browser only, and nothing is sent anywhere. Reset Everything clears it.

Should NSTP or PE be included?

It depends on the school. Many exclude NSTP from the GWA entirely while still requiring you to pass it. If you're unsure, compute it both ways and ask your registrar which one they use.

Is this an official GWA calculation?

No. This tool computes GWA based on the standard weighted-average formula, but only your school's registrar can issue your official GWA. Always confirm with your school for anything that affects honors, scholarships, or academic standing.
